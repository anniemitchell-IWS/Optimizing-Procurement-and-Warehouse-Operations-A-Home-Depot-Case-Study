# Business Context

## Company Overview

The Home Depot is the world's largest home improvement retailer, serving both do-it-yourself (DIY) customers and professional contractors (Pro customers). Through an extensive network of retail stores, distribution centers, and fulfillment facilities, the company provides building materials, tools, appliances, and specialty products to millions of customers.

In recent years, Home Depot has expanded its strategic focus on professional contractors by investing in supply chain modernization, digital capabilities, and specialized distribution networks designed to improve product availability, fulfillment speed, and customer service.

---

## The Pro Business Strategy

Professional contractors represent one of Home Depot's most important customer segments. Unlike DIY customers, Pro customers often purchase materials in larger quantities, require job-site deliveries, and depend on reliable inventory availability to keep construction projects on schedule.

To better serve this market, Home Depot has invested in expanding its Pro ecosystem through strategic acquisitions and operational improvements that strengthen its procurement, distribution, and fulfillment capabilities.

---

## Strategic Acquisitions

### SRS Distribution

In 2024, Home Depot acquired SRS Distribution, a leading specialty distributor serving professional contractors across the roofing, landscaping, pool, and exterior building products industries.

The acquisition expanded Home Depot's ability to serve specialized trade professionals while increasing its distribution network and product offerings.

---

### GMS

In 2025, Home Depot expanded its Pro business further through the acquisition of GMS (Gypsum Management & Supply), a leading distributor of specialty construction products including drywall, ceilings, steel framing, insulation, and related building materials.

This acquisition strengthened Home Depot's position within the commercial and residential construction markets while expanding its ability to support contractors throughout multiple phases of a construction project.

---

## Why This Matters

As organizations grow through acquisitions, operational complexity also increases.

While each business brings valuable expertise, integrating procurement, supplier management, warehouse operations, inventory visibility, and fulfillment processes becomes increasingly important to delivering a consistent customer experience.

Rather than viewing these acquisitions as independent businesses, they can also be viewed as opportunities to strengthen collaboration across the broader Pro Supply Network.

This case study examines how procurement and warehouse operations can support that continued integration through business process optimization and ERP principles.
